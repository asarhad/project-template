The rules this applies

  1. 90 days maximum in any rolling 180-day period. Not "90 days per half-year" — the window moves with you, one day at a time.
  2. The window looks backwards. For any date, count the days you were inside Schengen on that date and the 179 days before it. Anything older has already dropped out.
  3. Entry day and exit day each count as a full day, however briefly you were in the country. 28 Aug to 14 Sep is 18 days, not 17.
  4. All Schengen states share one 90-day budget. Greece, Spain, Italy, Croatia, Switzerland, Norway and the rest all draw on the same allowance. Ireland and Cyprus sit outside it.
  5. A trip is legal only if the limit holds on every day of it. Passing the test on your departure date is not enough — the figures below use the worst day of the trip.
  6. Days on a national long-stay (D) visa or a residence permit do not count toward the 90. Only leave those out of the trips list.

Regulation (EU) 2016/399 — Schengen Borders Code, Article 6(1)(b)
